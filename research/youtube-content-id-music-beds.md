# YouTube Content ID and usable music beds

Research for [YouTube Content ID and usable music beds](https://github.com/Perk4/cairn-essays/issues/3). Fetched 14 August 2026.

**Not a lock.** This note does not pick a music bed. Track lock lives on [Music bed without Content ID pain](https://github.com/Perk4/cairn-essays/issues/13).

Context this week: no human voice, no clone, no spoken VO. Captions carry prose. Flagship 16:9 plus 9:16 Shorts.

## What Content ID actually does

YouTube Content ID is an automated matcher, not a license checker. Copyright owners submit reference audio/video. Uploads are scanned against that database. A match creates a Content ID claim. The owner’s settings then **block**, **monetize** (ads, sometimes revenue share with the uploader), or **track** views. Actions can differ by country. ([How Content ID works](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370); [Learn about Content ID claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276); [About YouTube’s copyright management tools](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9245819))

Content ID is a YouTube product. A copyright removal request (takedown) is a legal process. A valid takedown applies a **copyright strike**. A Content ID claim is not a strike and usually does not affect channel standing. ([What is a copyright claim?](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7002106); [Understand copyright strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000))

YouTube’s own framing: it cannot decide ownership or whether a use is licensed or fair use. Disputes and appeals are reviewed by the claimant. ([Dispute a Content ID claim](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454))

### Claim effects that can eat a week

**Long-form (videos longer than three minutes).** Track or monetize claims stay viewable with the claim attached. “Monetize” is the most common policy for music claims. The uploader does not keep that ad revenue unless the owner shares it. ([Learn about Content ID claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276))

**Dispute clock.** Claimant has **30 days** to respond to a dispute. Options: release, reinstate, file a copyright removal request (strike risk), or let the claim expire. Invalid or abusive disputes can draw penalties. Credit, owning a copy, and “I didn’t monetize” are **not** valid dispute reasons. ([Dispute a Content ID claim](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454))

**Revenue hold.** If both sides want to monetize: dispute within **5 days** of the claim and revenue is held from day one; after 5 days, hold starts on the dispute date; do nothing and after 5 days held revenue goes to the claimant. Analytics for that period lag until after resolution. ([Monetization during Content ID disputes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7000961))

**Shorts 1–3 minutes with any active claim.** Blocked globally, not playable, not recommended, not eligible for monetization, regardless of the owner’s block/monetize/track policy. No channel strike. Remains blocked until the claim is resolved. Vertical/square uploads on or after 15 October 2024 up to three minutes are categorized as Shorts. ([Learn about Content ID claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276); [Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877); [YouTube Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220))

A licensed bed that still matches a Content ID reference can therefore: divert long-form revenue, start a 30-day claimant review, and **block** a 9:16 cut over one minute until someone safelists, disputes, or swaps audio.

## YouTube Audio Library terms

Official Help: [Use music and sound effects from the Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882). Studio path: YouTube Studio → Audio library, also `youtube.com/audiolibrary`.

Facts from that page:

- Tracks and SFX are described as royalty-free production music and **copyright-safe**.
- YPP members **may monetize** videos that use Audio Library music and SFX.
- “Copyright-safe music and sound effects downloaded from the Audio Library **won’t be claimed** by a rights holder through the Content ID system.”
- Two license types in the library:
  - **Attribution not required** — standard YouTube Audio Library license.
  - **Attribution required** — Creative Commons tracks. Credit must go in the video description. Studio can generate the attribution text. CC credit is still required even though a “Music in this video” block also appears on the watch page.
- New releases are added twice a month. Downloads are MP3.

YouTube’s disclaimer on the same page (verbatim meaning):

- **Only** Audio Library music and SFX are known to YouTube to be copyright-safe.
- YouTube is **not responsible** for issues from “royalty-free” music from YouTube channels or **other music libraries**.
- YouTube will not give legal guidance, including for off-platform music issues.

For Shorts, YouTube Help separately states you may use Shorts Audio Library songs (often up to 90 seconds in a 3-minute Short; some tracks 60 or 30) **or** royalty-free music from the YouTube Audio Library, and that Audio Library royalty-free music **won’t receive a Content ID claim**. Shorts over one minute with any other active claim are still blocked. ([Music eligibility for YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873); [Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877))

There is no separate public “Audio Library Terms of Service” document beyond this Help article and YouTube’s general Terms. The Help article is the first-party grant YouTube publishes for that catalog.

## Epidemic Sound

First-party pages fetched: [Music for YouTube videos](https://www.epidemicsound.com/youtube/music-for-youtube/), [What is a music license](https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/music-licensing/), [Safelisting](https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/music-licensing/what-is-safelisting/). Subscription legal URLs exist (`/policy/creator-subscription/`, `/policy/pro-subscription/`, `/policy/v2/legal/`) but returned a cookie wall in this session, so the grant below is taken from those marketing/help pages plus Epidemic Help Center articles indexed by Google (Cloudflare blocked a full Help fetch).

### What they grant

- They state they **own all rights** to catalog tracks (master, neighboring/performer, composition) and offer a **direct license**, not a classic split-rights royalty-free deal. ([Music licensing](https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/music-licensing/))
- An active subscription lets subscribers use catalog music/SFX in content published **online** (YouTube named) **and monetize**, “in accordance with the terms and conditions applicable to the subscription.” ([Music for YouTube](https://www.epidemicsound.com/youtube/music-for-youtube/); homepage FAQ quoted on that page)
- Content **published during an active subscription** (including free trial, per the safelisting page) **stays cleared forever** after cancel. New publishes after expiry are not cleared, even if the file was downloaded earlier. ([Safelisting](https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/music-licensing/what-is-safelisting/))
- **Safelisting** (connect the YouTube channel, or individual video URLs) is the mechanism that tells platforms the channel is licensed. They instruct connecting **before** publish. ([Safelisting](https://www.epidemicsound.com/how-it-works/music-licensing/what-is-safelisting/))
- Creator-plan Help (Google-indexed official article [Creator Plan](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26247405706386-Creator-Plan)): monetize YouTube with no view/follower cap; **one** account per platform (YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Twitch, one podcast RSS); Creator is for **non-commercial users** publishing on their own channels; **client work needs Pro**.

### What they do not grant / Content ID practice

- Download without a subscription or single-track license is not a publish license. ([Music for YouTube](https://www.epidemicsound.com/youtube/music-for-youtube/))
- **Listening-experience / music-only use is out of license.** Official Help [Can Epidemic Sound music be used for compilations or music-listening content?](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26254496789266-Can-Epidemic-Sound-music-be-used-for-compilations-or-music-listening-content): licensed to enhance original video or audio productions, **not** standalone listening. Forbidden examples include lyric videos; “uploading all or part of a track against still or **minimal visuals**” (e.g. an image with artist/track name); compilations; playlists; online radio. They reserve the right to monetize or take action against that use.
- Long-form Content ID from Epidemic, per [Why was a Content ID claim received from Epidemic Sound?](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26253712691730-Why-was-a-Content-ID-claim-received-from-Epidemic-Sound): YouTube detected their music and could not confirm a valid license, **or** the use is outside terms. Checklist they publish: valid license; correct channel connected or video URL linked; **not a listening experience**. Unlicensed use: they **monetize** (ads/revenue to Epidemic); they say the video is not removed, muted, or struck. Dispute only after safelist + license-at-publish; they tell users to dispute **within 5 days** to avoid lost ad revenue (aligns with YouTube’s hold rule).
- [Types of Copyright Claims on YouTube](https://help.epidemicsound.com/hc/en-us/articles/26251656597650-Types-of-Copyright-Claims-on-YouTube): Shorts claims on Epidemic catalog are described as **not** a long-form monetization claim; they say those claims do not affect visibility or ad revenue share and **need not be disputed**. That statement **conflicts with YouTube Help** for Shorts **over one minute**, which blocks any active claim. Epidemic’s YouTube marketing page claims you can “safely soundtrack and monetize your YouTube Shorts, even if they exceed one minute.” YouTube’s rule is the platform rule.

## Artlist

First-party license (full text fetched): [Artlist License](https://artlist.io/help-center/privacy-terms/artlist-license/). Help: [Understanding Artlist’s license](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29490991524253-Understanding-Artlist-s-license), [Understanding Artlist's Clearlist](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29648622402333-Understanding-Artlist-s-Clearlist), [Understanding copyright and Content ID claims](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29648852943901-Understanding-copyright-and-Content-ID-claims). License page marked effective **15 February 2026**.

### What they grant

- Paid subscription: download Assets and **integrate them into Projects that combine sound and visual media**. Publish and **monetize** on covered platforms while the sub is active.
- Projects **completed and published during an active subscription** remain cleared to use and monetize **forever** in the same media after expiry. New projects after expiry are not covered.
- **Social license:** one channel per platform (YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Twitch, plus one podcast). Monetize **only on those personal Channels**. No client work, no paid media, no third-party channel uploads. ([Artlist License — Social](https://artlist.io/help-center/privacy-terms/artlist-license/))
- **Pro / Teams:** more channels (Pro up to 3 per platform; Teams up to 5; Max Business unlimited). Client work and paid/promoted uses under Pro/Business as stated in the table on that page.
- **Clearlist:** connect YouTube channel and/or specific video URLs in the Artlist account. Artlist states its catalog is **registered with YouTube Content ID**. If the channel/video is on the Clearlist, YouTube is supposed to confirm the license and **not** flag. If it is not, a claim **may fire even if the file was downloaded legally**. Clearlist can only be updated while the subscription is active. ([Clearlist](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29648622402333-Understanding-Artlist-s-Clearlist))
- Monetization section of the license: if you do **not** add the channel or YouTube Project URL to the Clearlist, you **may receive claims from Artlist Ltd** and **will not be able to monetize** or remove ads. After adding, Artlist claims are cleared and monetization restored. **No reimbursement** for the unlisted period.

### What they do not grant

- License is **use, not ownership**. You may not register Assets or Projects in Content ID / Rights Manager, or put them in a trademark.
- **Not standalone audio.** Forbidden: music streaming, music channels, radio, “other ways of music or sound effects reproduction”; **static video**; **video loop animation** with Artlist music; **lyric video**. Assets may not be exploited as standalone content. Artlist reserves the right to decide gray cases. Same language in Social and Pro. ([Artlist License §§5 / Social §4](https://artlist.io/help-center/privacy-terms/artlist-license/))
- Help FAQ: playlist-style / music-only compilations / audio with static images / videos whose main purpose is presenting music for passive listening are **not permitted**. Sync license = background in a video project. ([Understanding Artlist’s license](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29490991524253-Understanding-Artlist-s-license))
- Public performance / broadcast: license **does not cover PRO / collecting-society royalties**; those may still be payable by the user, broadcaster, or platform. ([Artlist License §9 Pro](https://artlist.io/help-center/privacy-terms/artlist-license/))
- Artists may register with external PROs; Artlist says that does not change the royalty-free online license, but automated CID can still flag until Clearlist matches. ([Content ID claims Help](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29648852943901-Understanding-copyright-and-Content-ID-claims))

## CC0 and other Creative Commons

### CC0 1.0

Official deed: [CC0 1.0 Universal](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). Legal code: [legalcode.txt](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.txt).

- Affirmer waives copyright and related/neighboring rights to the extent allowed, worldwide, for **any purpose including commercial, advertising, or promotional**. Fallback: royalty-free irrevocable license for the same scope if the waiver fails.
- **No attribution requirement** in CC0 (unlike CC BY).
- Limitations: trademarks and patents are not waived. Affirmer gives **no warranties** (including title and non-infringement). Affirmer **disclaims responsibility for third-party rights** in the work. Creative Commons has **not verified** the copyright status of any CC0-labeled work. ([deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/); legal code §4)

CC0 is a copyright tool. It does **not** unregister a recording from Content ID, and it does not stop a different rights holder (or a false match) from claiming.

### Other CC licenses (not CC0)

From [CC license types](https://creativecommons.org/cc-licenses/) and deeds:

| License | Commercial (incl. YPP ads / monetary compensation) | Attribution | Adaptations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) | Allowed | Required | Allowed |
| CC BY-SA | Allowed | Required | Must share alike |
| CC BY-ND | Allowed | Required | Not allowed |
| [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) | **Not** allowed (“not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or **monetary compensation**”) | Required | Allowed |
| CC BY-NC-SA / CC BY-NC-ND | **Not** allowed | Required | SA / ND as named |

YouTube Audio Library’s “Attribution required” filter is this CC family, not CC0. Credit in the description is mandatory there even though the track is in YouTube’s own library. ([Audio Library Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882))

Giving CC credit is **not** a valid Content ID dispute reason by itself. ([Dispute a Content ID claim](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454))

## Similar sources (first-party only)

### Pixabay Content License (not CC0 for post-2019 uploads)

[Terms of Service](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/) (last updated 18 November 2024, fetched via Pixabay’s safesearch host): Content with published date **before 9 January 2019** is CC0. Later Content is a Pixabay **Content License**: irrevocable, worldwide, perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free, **commercial or non-commercial**, but **not standalone** distribution. Attribution not required. Pixabay **does not warrant** third-party consents; that burden is on the user. They may change the Terms, including licenses.

[Pixabay FAQ](https://pixabay.com/service/faq/) (official): music may be used in commercial videos if it is part of a larger creative work, not a standalone file. Same FAQ states **some contributors register tracks with Content ID**, so claims can fire **even when the Pixabay license allows the use**. Dispute with a Pixabay License Certificate where offered; tracks with a shield icon are CID-registered. FAQ also: avoid music-only / static-image uploads as more likely to be flagged.

YouTube’s Audio Library Help still treats Pixabay as an “other music library”: not known to YouTube to be copyright-safe. ([Audio Library Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882))

### Soundstripe

First-party docs (vendor, not YouTube): [Clearing YouTube claims with a code](https://docs.soundstripe.com/docs/clearing-youtube-claims-with-code); [Generate a YouTube code](https://docs.soundstripe.com/reference/generate-a-youtube-code-for-a-song). They state a subscription grants YouTube/social rights, but Content ID can still claim; clearance is via **channel listing** and/or a **single-use `youtube_code` in the description at upload**. Codes after the fact do not release monetization claims. Same pattern as Epidemic safelist / Artlist Clearlist: license and CID are separate systems.

## Facts that matter for a caption-led, no-VO essay

These are constraints for [Music bed without Content ID pain](https://github.com/Perk4/cairn-essays/issues/13), not a bed pick.

1. **License ≠ Content ID clearance.** YouTube only vouches for its own Audio Library. Epidemic, Artlist, Soundstripe, Pixabay, and CC0 all still live in a fingerprint database unless the owner safelists/clearlists or never registered the track.
2. **Safelist/Clearlist is operational, not optional**, for Epidemic/Artlist/Soundstripe if the catalog is in CID. Skipping it is how a licensed bed eats the week: monetize claim on long-form, **hard block** on Shorts >1 minute, 5-day revenue window, 30-day dispute.
3. **No spoken VO does not make the bed “music-only,” but the licenses police visuals.** Epidemic forbids still or **minimal** visuals. Artlist forbids static video, loop animation + music, lyric video, and playlist-style passive listening. A caption-led cut that is mostly still puppet frames plus a looping bed sits in the gray zone those vendors reserve the right to call a violation. Motion, scene changes, and on-screen prose that is the primary content (not the track) are the facts those licenses ask for.
4. **Shorts and long-form are different CID regimes.** Audio Library (attribution-not-required) is the only source YouTube says will not CID-claim either form. Epidemic’s “don’t dispute Shorts claims” line is not YouTube’s >1 minute block rule.
5. **CC BY-NC and friends are incompatible with monetization** as CC defines NonCommercial (monetary compensation). CC0 and CC BY allow commercial use **as copyright licenses**; they do not prevent CID or third-party claims.
6. **Credit does not clear a claim.**
